# Architecture Comparison Interpretation Across MVTec AD Categories

The architecture comparison was conducted to evaluate how different anomaly detection model configurations performed across the bottle, capsule and hazelnut categories. This experiment was included to provide a clearer justification for the anomaly detection architecture used in the dissertation project and to compare it against alternative model configurations under a consistent evaluation setup.

The comparison included PatchCore and PaDiM, both implemented through the Anomalib framework. PatchCore was evaluated using `resnet18`, `resnet50` and `wide_resnet50_2` feature-extraction backbones. PaDiM was evaluated using `resnet18` and `resnet50`, with `n_features` set to 100 for the ResNet50 configuration because this backbone required an explicit retained feature dimension in the installed Anomalib implementation. The evaluation focused on image-level AUROC and image-level F1-score because these metrics indicate how reliably each architecture separated normal and anomalous samples at inspection level.

Across the three evaluated categories, PatchCore consistently produced stronger image-level detection performance than PaDiM. For the bottle category, all three PatchCore configurations achieved an image AUROC of 1.0000 and an image F1-score of 0.9920. This indicates that the bottle defects were highly separable using PatchCore feature embeddings, and that increasing the PatchCore backbone capacity did not produce further image-level gains for this category. PaDiM also performed strongly on bottle, but its results remained slightly lower than PatchCore, with the ResNet18 configuration achieving an AUROC of 0.9976 and F1-score of 0.9839, while the ResNet50 configuration achieved an AUROC of 0.9952 and F1-score of 0.9841.

For the capsule category, the architecture comparison produced a clearer separation between the tested configurations. PatchCore with `resnet50` achieved the strongest result, with an image AUROC of 0.9936 and an image F1-score of 0.9818. PatchCore with `wide_resnet50_2` also performed strongly, with an AUROC of 0.9932 and F1-score of 0.9770, while PatchCore with `resnet18` achieved an AUROC of 0.9817 and F1-score of 0.9767. In contrast, PaDiM produced lower image-level AUROC values on capsule, with 0.8975 for ResNet18 and 0.9110 for ResNet50. Although the PaDiM F1-scores remained above 0.94, the lower AUROC values indicate weaker overall separation between normal and anomalous capsule samples.

The hazelnut category showed the largest difference between PatchCore and PaDiM. PatchCore with `resnet50` and `wide_resnet50_2` both achieved an image AUROC of 1.0000 and an image F1-score of 0.9928. PatchCore with `resnet18` was also strong, with an AUROC of 0.9986 and F1-score of 0.9787. PaDiM performed considerably lower on hazelnut, with the ResNet18 configuration achieving an AUROC of 0.7954 and F1-score of 0.8330, while the ResNet50 configuration achieved an AUROC of 0.7761 and F1-score of 0.7831. This suggests that PaDiM was less effective for this category under the tested feature and dimensionality settings.

Based on these results, PatchCore was selected as the main anomaly detection architecture for the dissertation pipeline. More specifically, PatchCore with a `resnet50` backbone provided the strongest overall balance across the tested categories. It matched the best PatchCore performance on bottle, achieved the strongest capsule result, and matched the best hazelnut result. The `wide_resnet50_2` configuration also performed strongly, particularly on bottle and hazelnut, but it did not improve upon the ResNet50 configuration in the reported image-level results. Therefore, PatchCore with `resnet50` offers a suitable balance between detection strength and architectural efficiency for the current prototype.

The performance difference can be explained by the way PatchCore and PaDiM model normality. PatchCore stores locally aware patch-level feature embeddings from normal training images and uses nearest-neighbour distance in feature space to identify anomalous regions. Its use of memory-bank modelling and coreset sampling allows it to preserve representative normal feature patterns while reducing redundancy. This makes it effective for detecting subtle deviations from normal industrial product appearance. PaDiM, by contrast, models normal patch embeddings statistically using a multivariate Gaussian distribution. This approach can be effective, but it may be less robust when the normal feature distribution is complex or when defects produce feature variations that overlap with normal appearance.

The architecture comparison therefore supports the use of PatchCore as the preferred anomaly detection branch within the proposed risk-aware inspection framework. The selected architecture was not chosen solely because of a single result; it was selected because it showed consistently strong image-level anomaly detection across multiple MVTec AD categories and remained compatible with the wider pipeline, including localisation, semantic interpretation, confidence fusion, risk classification, human-review gating and quality disposition logic.

These results are important for the dissertation because they show that the selected model architecture was evaluated against alternative configurations rather than assumed to be suitable. The comparison provides evidence that PatchCore, particularly with the ResNet50 backbone, offers the strongest image-level detection performance among the tested configurations. However, the architecture decision should still be interpreted alongside the localisation and operational review results, because the overall aim of the project is not only to classify anomalous images, but to support traceable and risk-aware inspection decisions.
